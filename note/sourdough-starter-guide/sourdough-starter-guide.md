# Sourdough Starter Guide

#baking #fermentation · Part of [[Bread Basics|the bread basics series]]. See also [[Flour Types#Whole grains]].

A starter is flour and water that wild yeast and lactic bacteria have colonised. Kept warm and fed on a schedule, it raises bread without any shop-bought yeast.

> [!TIP] Start with rye
> Whole rye flour carries more wild yeast than white flour, so a rye starter usually bubbles a day or two sooner.

## Day one to day seven

Mix 50 g flour with 50 g water in a clean jar, cover loosely and leave at about 24 °C. Each day discard all but 50 g and feed it again.

Feed once a day for the first week, then twice a day once it doubles within eight hours. Keep the jar out of direct sun. ^feeding

> [!WARNING] Hooch is not mould
> A grey liquid on top means the starter is hungry: pour it off and feed. Pink or orange streaks are mould; throw it away and start again.

## Is it ready?

- It doubles within 4 to 8 hours of a feed
- It smells sour and yeasty, like yoghurt
- A spoonful floats in water[^float]

> [!QUESTION] Fridge or counter?
> Bake weekly or less? Keep it in the fridge and feed it the night before baking.

[^float]: The float test is a rough guide; a wet starter can sink even when active.
